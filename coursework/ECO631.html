ECO 631: Applied Policy Methods

Spring 2025

Coursework

This project re-calculates and stress-tests a government cost–benefit analysis using the authors’ own stated assumptions. It was selected because it highlights independent verification, present value modeling, and the identification of potential overstatements in reported benefit figures.

Portfolio Assignment: View Report (PDF)  |  View Calculations (PDF)

Learning Objectives

Identify the costs, benefits and tradeoffs involved in economic policy making

  • Derive research questions concerning policy from conceptual and theoretical foundations.
  • Integrate institutional context into assessment of policy.
  • Identify private and social impacts of policy.

Compare and contrast alternative economic policy assessments to reach a defensible recommendation

  • Characterize differences in methodology and data used to assess policy.
  • Assess weaknesses and strengths of alternative approaches to policy assessment.
  • Propose and support an assessment of a policy-related body of research.

Present the motivation, methods and conclusions from applied economic research

  • Describe and motivate an applied economic research question.
  • Identify and explain why particular data and methods are appropriate for a given empirical analysis.
  • Interpret and communicate results of an empirical analysis.

Reflection

ECO 631 deepened my understanding of benefit-cost analysis and formal program evaluation. Through ten structured written critiques and a final exam paper, the course emphasized evaluating assumptions, identifying methodological weaknesses, and distinguishing between private and social impacts. Each assignment required close reading of applied policy studies and careful analysis of how benefits and costs were measured, discounted, and interpreted.

The repeated focus on identifying tradeoffs and assessing alternative policy methodologies strengthened my ability to compare competing evaluation approaches and reach defensible recommendations. Rather than simply calculating metrics such as NPV or benefit-cost ratios, the course emphasized questioning measurement choices, recognizing potential double counting, evaluating counterfactual assumptions, and critiquing sensitivity analyses. This sharpened my capacity to assess policy research beyond its surface conclusions.

The structured writing component of the course also advanced my ability to clearly present motivation, methods, and conclusions from applied economic research. By producing concise written critiques under word limits, I developed greater precision in communicating economic reasoning. ECO 631 therefore contributed significantly to my ability to analyze, evaluate, and communicate applied policy research in a professional context.
