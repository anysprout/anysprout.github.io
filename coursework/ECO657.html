ECO 657: Managerial Economics

Spring 2026

Learning Objectives

By the end of the course, you will be able to:

  1. Setup and solve the fundamental microeconomic models.
  2. Understand the economic intuition underlying these models.
  3. Analyze, interpret and communicate the results that these models produce.
  4. Apply these models to real-world managerial problems.
  5. Use data to inform business decision making.

Reflection

ECO 657 helped me understand how firms use economic theory to make day-to-day business decisions and how market conditions shape longer-term planning. The course made concepts like pricing, production, competition, and strategic behavior feel much more practical by tying them to the kinds of choices firms actually face. It also deepened my understanding of how different market structures lead to different incentives, constraints, and outcomes.

More than anything, the course helped me build better intuition around competition and how a firm’s decisions are shaped by the behavior of its rivals. That made managerial economics feel less like a set of isolated models and more like a framework for thinking through real business problems. Because the course work was completed primarily through Pearson and online assessments, it did not lend itself well to selecting a downloadable assignment for this portfolio. Even so, the course was valuable in strengthening my ability to apply microeconomic reasoning to practical decision making.
