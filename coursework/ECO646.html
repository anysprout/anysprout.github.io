Learning Objectives

By the end of this course, you will be able to:

  • Identify the key determinants of long-run per capita income and economic growth.
  • Explain the theoretical foundations and empirical implications of modern growth models.
  • Describe the main features of modern business cycles.
  • Apply short-run macroeconomic models to analyze fluctuations in economic activity.
  • Assess how individual behavior and expectations influence the effectiveness of macroeconomic policy.
  • Understand how macroeconomic data is collected, compiled, and interpreted.
  • Identify the major agencies responsible for producing macroeconomic data.

Reflection

ECO 646 provided a structured analytical framework for understanding both long-run economic growth and short-run macroeconomic fluctuations. The course emphasized formal modeling, including growth theory and business cycle analysis, and required applying these frameworks to interpret macroeconomic data and policy developments.

Through problem sets, unit exams, and applied projects using macroeconomic data, I strengthened my ability to connect theoretical models to empirical evidence. The course reinforced the importance of understanding the assumptions underlying growth models, the role of expectations in shaping macroeconomic outcomes, and the interaction between policy design and economic behavior.

By integrating theoretical reasoning with data interpretation, ECO 646 enhanced my ability to analyze macroeconomic phenomena in a disciplined and structured way. The emphasis on identifying determinants of long-run income, interpreting business cycle dynamics, and assessing policy effectiveness deepened my overall economic analytical toolkit and strengthened my capacity to evaluate macroeconomic policy debates.
