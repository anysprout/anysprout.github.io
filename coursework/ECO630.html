Spring 2026

Learning Objectives

  • Provide an introduction to the methods used by health economists to evaluate health care technologies and programs.
  • Learn how to critique and use health economic evaluations conducted by others.
  • Use some common health economic evaluation tools and methods.

Reflection

ECO 630 gave me an introductory understanding of health economics through work that felt structured, practical, and unfamiliar in a useful way. My final project (in-progress) compares provider-delivered and specialist-delivered alcohol screening and brief intervention in primary care, which required cost analysis, outcome measurement, and cost-effectiveness analysis from a healthcare system perspective. In doing that, I developed a much stronger appreciation for transparency and justification of assumptions. Since real-world analysis rarely provides perfect data, I had to make careful decisions about which evidence to use, how to explain proxies and estimates, and when an analysis was strong enough to be useful even if it could not resolve every uncertainty.

The course also helped me think more clearly about audience and purpose. Cost-effectiveness analysis is meant to inform a decision-maker who needs the right information presented in a credible and readable way. That pushed me to think harder about what a stakeholder actually needs to know, how much detail is necessary, and how to communicate findings without losing the logic behind them. It also gave me more respect for standardized evidence and shared methodological conventions, since those make it possible to compare findings across studies and build on prior research. More broadly, ECO 630 helped me navigate dense and unfamiliar literature with more confidence while producing analysis that is careful, defensible, and useful.
