ECO 641: Microeconomics I

Fall 2025

Coursework

This project evaluates the economic impact of eliminating North Carolina’s gasoline tax using supply and demand modeling, incidence analysis, and welfare calculations. I selected it because it demonstrates applied microeconomic reasoning, quantitative policy evaluation, and the ability to move from equilibrium analysis to broader public finance considerations.

Portfolio Assignment: View Assignment (PDF)  |  View Write Up (PDF)

Learning Objectives

Consumer Theory (Demand)

  1. Solve utility maximization problems.
  2. Derive individual demand functions from utility functions and aggregate the individual demand functions into market demand functions.
  3. Derive and interpret the meaning of the own-price elasticity of demand, the cross-price elasticity of demand and the income elasticity of demand.

Producer Theory (Supply)

  1. Find the cost-minimizing combinations of inputs to produce a given level of output.
  2. Derive a firm’s long run cost function from the firm’s production function.
  3. Find the profit maximizing output level for a price-taking firm.

Market Efficiency Theory (Demand + Supply)

  1. Explain the concepts of consumer surplus and producer surplus and interpret the welfare effects of price changes.
  2. Derive the market response of a competitive industry to either a change in demand or a change in supply.
  3. Apply the concepts of Pareto efficiency and the efficiency of markets.

Reflection

ECO 641 strengthened my formal microeconomic foundation by emphasizing mathematical optimization and its relationship to graphical and conceptual economic reasoning. The course required solving constrained optimization problems, deriving demand and cost functions, and analyzing firm behavior under competitive market assumptions. This reinforced the connection between theoretical modeling and economic interpretation.

Working through consumer and producer theory deepened my understanding of elasticity, cost minimization, profit maximization, and welfare analysis. Rather than treating these topics as abstract concepts, the course emphasized deriving results formally and interpreting them economically. This sharpened my ability to move between mathematical derivation and policy-relevant intuition.

The focus on market efficiency and welfare analysis also strengthened my broader analytical framework for evaluating policy interventions. By grounding economic reasoning in formal optimization and equilibrium analysis, ECO 641 enhanced my ability to assess tradeoffs, efficiency implications, and distributional outcomes in applied economic contexts.
