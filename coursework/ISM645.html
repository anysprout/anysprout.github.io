ISM 645: Principles of Predictive Analytics

Fall 2024

Coursework

Portfolio Assignment: View Full Project Notebook on GitHub →

Learning Objectives

  • Demonstrate an understanding of the principles of predictive analytics.
  • Identify, design, and assess different predictive analytics methodologies.
  • Prepare and formulate data collection, preprocessing, and exploration for analytical purposes.
  • Apply and assess different predictive analytic models and techniques.
  • Evaluate organizational implications of the use and implementation of predictive analytics.
  • Demonstrate proficiency in the use of analytics software.

Reflection

In ISM 645, I developed a working understanding of the predictive analytics pipeline—from defining a problem and preparing data to training models, evaluating performance, and interpreting results. The portfolio assignment required me to move beyond “running a model” and instead think through the end-to-end decisions that determine whether a predictive tool is useful: selecting appropriate features, avoiding overfitting, choosing evaluation metrics that match the problem, and translating model performance into practical implications. This helped reinforce an important lesson for my broader program goals: predictive work is not just technical execution, but a series of judgment calls that should be aligned with the decision the model is meant to support.

This course directly supports my short-term goal of strengthening quantitative and applied data skills in a way that is legible to employers and collaborators. Rather than treating modeling as a purely academic exercise, ISM 645 pushed me to frame results in terms of tradeoffs and outcomes—what improves accuracy, what introduces bias, what is interpretable, and what is operationally feasible. That emphasis maps closely to the kind of work I want to do professionally: applied economic and data analysis that informs strategy, evaluation, and resource allocation in mission-driven settings.

Long-term, I expect to apply these skills in contexts that focus on economic empowerment and community development—where measuring impact and targeting interventions responsibly matters. Predictive tools can support better decisions, but only if the analyst understands the limitations of the data and communicates uncertainty honestly. ISM 645 strengthened my ability to build models with that mindset: grounded in evaluation, transparent about assumptions, and oriented toward real-world use.
