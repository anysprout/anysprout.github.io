Learning Objectives

Upon successful completion of the course, students will be able to:

  1. Estimate and interpret simple and multiple linear regression models, as well as their nonlinear extensions.
  2. Incorporate qualitative information into regression models through dummy variables.
  3. Conduct statistical inference on regression model parameters, including inference that is robust to heteroskedasticity.
  4. Detect and resolve common issues in econometric analysis, such as omitted variables and choice of specification.

Reflection

ECO 643 gave me a much deeper understanding of econometrics by strengthening my grasp of the mathematical foundations behind regression models and hypothesis testing. This was the course where ideas like coefficients, standard errors, confidence intervals, and formal tests started to feel more connected. Instead of treating regression output as something to decode after the fact, I developed a better understanding of what the model was doing, what the results meant, and how statistical reasoning supports economic interpretation.

While this course did not include a formal final project, I selected an assignment that reflects that development. It involved SAS-based regression analysis, transformed variables, joint hypothesis testing, and correlation checks tied to specification, multicollinearity, and omitted variable bias. What mattered most to me was learning to look beyond goodness-of-fit alone when evaluating a model. A stronger fit can be useful, but specification also has to be guided by economic theory and interpretability. The course also deepened my understanding of SAS and made me more comfortable moving between code, mathematics, and applied econometric reasoning.
