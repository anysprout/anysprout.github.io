ECO 664: Time Series and Forecasting

Spring 2025

Coursework

This project applies advanced time series forecasting methods to daily Steam player counts, including manual SARIMA specification, hierarchical reconciliation (Bottom-Up and MinT), and neural network autoregression. I selected it because it demonstrates model selection, diagnostic evaluation, and critical assessment of forecast realism in a dynamic real-world dataset.

Learning Objectives

  • Articulate the forecasting process and express its value to experts and non-experts.
  • Evaluate accuracy of forecasts.
  • Identify and implement benchmark and principal forecasting methods.
  • Communicate forecast results.
  • Develop proficiency in R.

Reflection

ECO 664 was my favorite course in the program because it showed me how quantitative analysis could connect directly to something I already cared about. Working with Steam player-count data made the course feel more alive to me than a generic forecasting exercise would have. It gave me the chance to bring a real personal interest into serious analytical work, and that made me more invested in both the modeling process and the interpretation of the results.

This course also pushed my programming skills further by introducing me to R and giving me space to grow more independent in how I approached analysis. As I worked through different forecasting methods, I got to the point where I built an algorithm to process hundreds of ARIMA combinations so I could identify the best fit against test data. That experience taught me a great deal about model selection, diagnostics, and the practical logic of time series analysis. More than anything, it showed me that this kind of work has direct relevance to the kinds of career paths that interest me, especially those involving behavioral patterns, dynamic systems, and data-informed decision making.
